CDC COVID-19 VACCINE MYTHS AND FACTS
Source: Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)
Fact-Checking Common Misconceptions

===================
MYTH VS FACT
===================

Myth: A COVID-19 vaccine can make you sick with COVID-19.
Fact: None of the authorized COVID-19 vaccines in the United States contain the live virus that causes COVID-19, so they cannot cause you to get sick with the disease itself. Any symptoms like fever or feeling run down after vaccination are normal signs that your body is building protection.

Myth: COVID-19 vaccines contain microchips or are used for tracking.
Fact: COVID-19 vaccines do not contain microchips. Vaccines are designed to stimulate your immune system to fight disease, not to track your movement.

Myth: Natural immunity from having COVID-19 is better than immunity from vaccination.
Fact: Getting a COVID-19 vaccination is a safer and more dependable way to build immunity. Vaccination often leads to a more predictable immune response and can provide added protection even for those who have already had COVID-19. The protective effect of a COVID-19 vaccine wears off after about six months, and immunity from infection fades after around three months due to declining antibody levels and viral mutations.

Myth: COVID-19 vaccines affect fertility or menstrual cycles.
Fact: There is currently no evidence that COVID-19 vaccines cause fertility problems in women or men. The CDC recommends COVID-19 vaccination for individuals who are pregnant, trying to get pregnant, or might become pregnant in the future, as well as their partners.

Myth: COVID-19 vaccines can change your DNA.
Fact: COVID-19 vaccines deliver instructions to teach the body how to build protection against the virus. They do not alter your DNA, and the mRNA is quickly broken down in your cells.

Myth: The COVID-19 vaccine from last year or a prior infection means you don't need a new vaccine.
Fact: The protective effect of COVID-19 vaccines and natural immunity decreases over time. The 2024-2025 COVID-19 vaccine has been updated to offer better protection against currently circulating strains of the virus. Getting an updated vaccine is the best way to protect against severe illness, hospitalization, death, and long COVID.

Myth: If you are healthy, your body will naturally fight off COVID-19 without the vaccine.
Fact: While the risk of severe illness is higher for certain groups, young and healthy individuals can still experience severe illness or death from COVID-19. The vaccine significantly reduces this risk.

Myth: COVID-19 no longer causes severe illness or death.
Fact: Patients are still dying in hospitals from COVID-19 complications. While the risk of severe cases is higher for the elderly, immunocompromised, or those with chronic conditions, anyone infected carries some risk.

Myth: COVID-19 vaccines were rushed and are not safe.
Fact: COVID-19 vaccines were approved by the FDA after rigorous testing in tens of thousands of patients during clinical trials, proving them to be safe and effective at preventing serious illness, hospitalization, and death. Serious side effects are extremely rare.

Myth: Influenza (flu) vaccines also protect against COVID-19.
Fact: Flu vaccines protect only against influenza, and COVID-19 vaccines protect only against COVID-19. You need to receive a COVID-19 vaccine for protection against the COVID-19 virus.

Myth: It is better to catch COVID-19 than to get the COVID-19 vaccine.
Fact: COVID-19 can be very serious, leading to pneumonia, organ failure, blood clots, hospitalization, and even death. Some individuals experience long-lasting symptoms. Vaccination is the best way to protect against it.
